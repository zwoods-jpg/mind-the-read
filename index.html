The problem

Solvers taught a generation to play nobody in particular.

Modern poker training hands you an isolated spot with no history, no opponent, and no session — flashcards with beautiful math behind them.

But almost nobody loses money because they missed an equilibrium frequency on the turn. They lose because they can’t read the person across the table and adjust. And at the stakes most people actually play, the table is full of opponents making enormous, repeatable, obvious mistakes — mistakes a balanced strategy politely declines to punish.

There is a second gap nobody fills. Live games run straddles, everyone limps, and pots go four ways. Solvers barely address multiway play, so the players who study hardest online can still look lost in a cardroom.

The arithmetic nobody teaches

Why bluffing dies in a multiway pot.

To win a bluff, every single opponent has to fold. Say a two-thirds-pot bluff needs to work 40% of the time. Here is what each player has to fold for that to be true:

Against 1 player
40%
Against 2 players
63%
Against 3 players
74%
Against 4 players
80%

Nobody at a live table folds 80% of the time. That is not a stylistic opinion about playing tight — it is the reason multiway bluffing has to nearly disappear, and why your value bets should get bigger instead, not smaller.

The trainer teaches this by making you do it, then showing you the number.

What it does

A hundred players. Eight types. One of them is reading you back.

Opponents with identities, not difficulty sliders

Ingrid waits all night for aces. Priya came to see cards, not to fold. Aisha throws chips at every flop. A hundred players rotate through the table, and they all fall into eight recognisable types. You are never asked to remember a person — you are asked to recognise a pattern, which is the skill that transfers to a room full of strangers.

A coach that grades the read, not just the odds

Because the trainer knows each opponent’s true range, it can compute exactly what the most profitable decision is against that player — then tell you whether you found it. Not what a balanced strategy would do. What actually wins against the person in front of you.

They adjust to you

The table tracks how often you fold. Fold too much and the thinking players start betting at you with nothing — a shark inside an orbit, a calling station never. How fast someone adapts is itself a tell you learn to read.

Quirks the statistics cannot show you

Two players can have identical numbers and still play nothing alike. One fires a second barrel every time; one gives up on the turn. One saves every bluff for the river; another has never bluffed a river in their life. One bets small with a merged range and one bets big with a polarised one — a sizing tell you can learn to read. None of it appears on a stats readout. You have to watch.

Two opponents built to beat the readout

The Trap looks passive on any HUD, because he never bets his good hands — he checks them and raises you when you bet for him. The Gear Shifter changes style every few hands on purpose, so the session average describes a player who is not sitting there any more. Neither can be identified from statistics. That is the point.

The live game: straddles, limpers, four-way pots

The parts of poker that solvers skip and other trainers ignore — and the parts you will actually face at $1/$2. Isolation sizing over limpers, why a straddle halves everyone’s stack, and why one pair is a bluff-catcher when four people saw the flop.

A leak report that refuses to guess

It names your three biggest leaks and what each costs. Postflop costs are measured from real expected value. Preflop costs are labelled estimates. Read leaks get no dollar figure at all, because any number there would be invented. It will not judge you on results, and it says so.
